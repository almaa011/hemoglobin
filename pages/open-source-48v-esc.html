The Best Open-Source Project For A 48V 3-Phase Motor Driver

A Hardware Engineer's Survey Of The Open ESC Landscape — Then A Build-Level Deep Dive On What It Actually Takes To Drive Three Phases From A 48V Bus.

Read The Verdict
VESC moteus ODrive SimpleFOC

2026-08-01

TL;DR

For A 48V Motor, Start From VESC. Its hardware is released under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 and its firmware under GPLv3, so schematics, PCB, BOM and code are all there to fabricate a proven design — and the VESC 6 MkVI is rated for the full 6–60V band that a 12S / 48V pack lives in. If you need a fully-permissive licence (hardware and firmware both Apache 2.0) for a robotics or servo build, moteus n1 (10–54V) is the cleaner reuse. Avoid leaning on ODrive as your "open" base — its newer hardware and firmware have gone closed — and note that SimpleFOC, while the easiest on-ramp to FOC, ships no board rated above 35V, so it is not a 48V ESC on its own. Whatever the base, the 48V-specific work is the same: MOSFETs rated 80–100V (never 48V), tight dead-time to stop shoot-through, and a low-inductance power-loop layout.

Four Projects Claim To Be Open. Only Two Really Are At 48V.

01 The Verdict

Openness And Voltage Are The Two Axes That Matter. A Project Fails If Its Hardware Files Are Closed, Or If No Board In Its Family Reaches A 48V Bus. Two Of These Four Fail On One Axis Each.

WATCH OUT

SimpleFOC Is A FOC Library, Not A 48V ESC. Its official boards top out at 35V (Shield / Mini), 30V (Drive) and 24V (PowerShield)[9]. It is superb for learning FOC on a bench motor, but to run it at 48V you must design your own power stage — at which point the project gives you the brain, not the finished driver.

03 The Power Stage

What You're Actually Building

An ESC Is A Three-Phase Inverter Plus A Commutation Brain. The power stage is six switches — three half-bridges — that chop the DC bus into three phase currents; the controller decides which switch is on when.

The two control styles map onto circuits you already know. This is general engineering knowledge, not tied to one source:

  • Six-Step (Trapezoidal) — Only Two Of The Three Phases Are Energised At Any Instant; The Controller Steps Through Six States Per Electrical Revolution. Simple And Cheap, But Torque Ripples At Each Commutation.
  • FOC (Field-Oriented Control) — All Three Phases Are Driven With Sinusoidal Current, Regulated In A Rotating Reference Frame. Smoother And More Efficient On A Sinusoidal-Back-EMF Motor, But It Needs Current Sensing And More Compute.
The Six-Switch Three-Phase Bridge
The Power Stage Inside Every ESC On This Page
Switch On (Conducting) Switch Off Phase Node A / B / C

One Instant Of Six-Step Commutation: High-Side Q1 And Low-Side Q6 Conduct, So Current Flows DC+ → Phase A → Motor → Phase C → DC−. Firing Q1 And Q2 (Same Leg) At Once Is Shoot-Through — A Dead Short Across The Bus.

04 Build Deep-Dive

Three Decisions Make It A 48V Driver

Gate Driver Headroom, MOSFET Voltage Rating And Commutation Scheme Are The Whole Job. Get These Three Right And The Rest Is Layout Discipline.

The Gate Driver

DRV8301 / DRV8323 Give 6–60V With 1.7A Source And 2.3A Sink[10] — 48V Sits Near The Top. The DRV8353 Runs 9–100V With A Charge Pump For 100% Duty[11], Leaving Real Transient Margin.

The MOSFETs

Size VDS To At Least 1.5× The Bus — For 48V That Means 80V, Often 100V Parts[12]. Never Fit A 48V-Rated Device To A 48V Rail.

The Control Scheme

Six-Step Is Least Code And Least Sensing; FOC Is Smoother But Needs Per-Phase Current Sense. VESC And moteus Both Ship FOC, Which Is Why Cloning Beats Writing Commutation Yourself.

05 Where 48V Bites

48V Bus The Gotchas That Kill Boards

48V Is High Enough To Destroy Silicon In Milliseconds And Low Enough To Tempt You Into Thin Margins. These Are General Engineering Failure Modes, Listed So A First Build Survives.

1 Shoot-Through
4 Regen Overvoltage
2 Dead-Time Trade-Off
5 Bus Cap Ripple Current
3 Power-Loop Inductance
6 Bootstrap At High Duty
SHOOT-THROUGH

If Both FETs In One Leg Conduct Together, The Bus Shorts To Ground Through Them And They Fail Almost Instantly. The Fix Is Dead-Time — A Deliberate Gap Where Both Are Off During The Hand-Off. Too Little Risks Cross-Conduction; Too Much Forces Body-Diode Conduction And Wastes Efficiency. Use The Gate Driver's Configurable Dead-Time Rather Than Relying On The MCU.

  • Power-Loop Layout — The High-di/dt Loop (Bus Cap → High-Side FET → Low-Side FET → Back To Cap) Must Be Physically Tiny. Wide Copper Pours, Minimal Loop Area, Many Parallel Vias, And Decoupling Right At The Driver. Loop Inductance Is What Turns A 48V Bus Into 70V+ Spikes.
  • Regen Overvoltage — A Decelerating Motor Becomes A Generator And Pushes The Bus Above The Battery Voltage. Your FET And Capacitor Ratings Must Absorb That, Or You Need A Brake Resistor Or Clamp.
  • Bus Capacitance — Size The DC-Link Caps For Ripple Current, Not Just Voltage. Undersized Bulk Capacitance Lets The Bus Sag And Ring Under Fast Current Steps.
WHY THE CHARGE PUMP MATTERS

A Plain Bootstrap Supply Cannot Hold The High-Side Gate On At 100% Duty — It Relies On The Low-Side Turning On To Refresh The Bootstrap Cap. A Charge Pump (As On The DRV8353[11]) Keeps The High-Side FET Fully Enhanced Even At Full Duty, Which Matters When A Motor Is Loaded Near Top Speed. This Is General Power-Electronics Knowledge Reflected In The Datasheet Feature Set.

06 Licensing

The "Open" Traps

Open Firmware Does Not Mean You Can Ship It Under Any Name. Two traps catch people building on these projects.

  • VESC Trademark ≠ Copyright Licence — The GPLv3 Firmware And CC-BY-SA Hardware Do Not Grant Rights To The VESC® Name. Its Trademark Policy States That An Open-Source Copyright Licence "Does Not Include An Implied Right Or License To Use A Trademark," And That The Mark Must Not Appear On Cloned Products[6]. Build The Hardware Freely; Do Not Brand It "VESC."
  • ODrive Is Closing — Older v3.x Firmware Is Open, But The Source For The Current Pro / S1 / Micro Generation "Is Currently Not Publicly Available" (Access Only Under NDA), And The Matching Hardware Files Are No Longer Released[7]. Fine As A Product; Not A Foundation You Can Fully Own.

07 Open Questions

What The Research Couldn't Pin Down

A Few Facts Stayed Below The Bar Of A Verifiable Primary Source and are flagged rather than guessed.

CONFLICT — VESC 4.12 GATE DRIVER

Sources Disagreed On The Exact Part In The Older VESC 4.12: One Cited A Generic DRV8301, Another The DRV8302. Both Are The Same 6–60V TI Family; The Canonical VESC 4.12 Part Is The DRV8302. Not Load-Bearing Here, Since The 48V Recommendation Is The VESC 6 MkVI, Not The 4.12.

UNRESOLVED NUMBERS

The Exact Regen Overvoltage Peak For A Given 48V Motor And Controller, The Minimum Bootstrap Hold-Up Time At 48V, And Whether VESC Publishes Complete Manufacturing Gerbers Versus Schematics Only — None Resolved To A Quotable Primary Source. Treat The Regen And Bootstrap Points As Qualitative Design Warnings, Not Design Numbers.
