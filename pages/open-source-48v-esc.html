The Best Open-Source Project For A 48V 3-Phase Motor Driver

A Hardware Engineer's Survey Of The Open ESC Landscape — VESC, moteus, ODrive, SimpleFOC — Then A Build-Level Deep Dive On What It Actually Takes To Drive A Three-Phase Motor From A 48V Bus.

2026-08-01

TL;DR

For A 48V Motor, Start From VESC. Its hardware is released under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 and its firmware under GPLv3, so schematics, PCB, BOM and code are all there to fabricate a proven design — and the VESC 6 MkVI is rated for the full 6–60V band that a 12S / 48V pack lives in. If you need a fully-permissive licence (hardware and firmware both Apache 2.0) for a robotics or servo build, moteus n1 (10–54V) is the cleaner reuse. Avoid leaning on ODrive as your "open" base — its newer hardware and firmware have gone closed — and note that SimpleFOC, while the easiest on-ramp to FOC, ships no board rated above 35V, so it is not a 48V ESC on its own. Whatever the base, the 48V-specific work is the same: MOSFETs rated 80–100V (never 48V), tight dead-time to stop shoot-through, and a low-inductance power-loop layout.

01 The Landscape

What You're Actually Building

An ESC Is A Three-Phase Inverter Plus A Commutation Brain. The power stage is six switches — three half-bridges — that chop the DC bus into three phase currents; the controller decides which switch is on when.

The two control styles map cleanly onto circuits you already know. This is general engineering knowledge, not tied to one source:

  • Six-Step (Trapezoidal) — Only Two Of The Three Phases Are Energised At Any Instant; The Controller Steps Through Six States Per Electrical Revolution. Simple, Cheap, But Torque Ripples At Each Commutation.
  • FOC (Field-Oriented Control) — All Three Phases Are Driven With Sinusoidal Current, Regulated In A Rotating Reference Frame. Smoother And More Efficient On A Sinusoidal-Back-EMF Motor, But It Needs Current Sensing And More Compute.

Every open project below is really a packaging of the same three-half-bridge power stage plus one of these two brains. The differences that matter for you are voltage rating, how open the design truly is, and how much you have to build yourself.

One Instant Of Six-Step Commutation: High-Side Q1 And Low-Side Q6 Conduct, So Current Flows DC+ → Phase A → Motor → Phase C → DC−. Firing Q1 And Q2 (Same Leg) At Once Is Shoot-Through — A Dead Short Across The Bus.

02 The Recommendation

Start From VESC

VESC Is The Best All-Round Open Base For A 48V Motor. It is the only project on this list that is genuinely open at both layers and explicitly rated for the 48V band, with a mature toolchain and the largest community.

The hardware is licensed Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International[4], and the firmware is GPLv3[3] — so schematics, PCB layout, BOM and source are all published to fabricate a proven design rather than invent one.

The VESC 6 MkVI is rated 6–60V (safe for 3S–12S LiPo), with input capped around 52V and voltage spikes that must not exceed 60V — squarely covering a 48V-class 12S pack[5].

SimpleFOC Is A FOC Library, Not A 48V ESC. Its official boards top out at 35V (SimpleFOCShield / Mini), 30V (Drive) and 24V (PowerShield)[9]. It is superb for learning FOC on a bench motor, but to run it at 48V you must design your own power stage — at which point the project gives you the brain, not the finished driver.

03 Build Deep-Dive

Making It Drive 48V

Whether You Clone VESC Or Design Fresh, The 48V Power Stage Comes Down To Three Component Decisions. Get the gate driver, the MOSFETs and the control scheme right and the rest is layout discipline.

1 — The Gate Driver

Pick A Driver Whose Supply Range Clears Your Bus With Headroom. Two families dominate open ESCs, both from TI:

  • DRV8301 / DRV8302 / DRV8323 — 6–60V Operating Range (65V Absolute Max), ~1.7A Source / 2.3A Sink Gate Drive On The DRV8301[10]. This Is The Classic VESC-Class Part. A 48V Bus Sits Near The Top Of Its Window, So Transient Margin Is Thin.
  • DRV8353 — 9–100V Operating Range With An Integrated Charge Pump For 100% PWM Duty[11]. For A fresh 48V Design This Is The Safer Pick: The Bus Sits Mid-Range, Leaving Comfortable Room For Ringing.
WHY THE CHARGE PUMP MATTERS

A Plain Bootstrap Supply Cannot Hold The High-Side Gate On At 100% Duty — It Relies On The Low-Side Turning On To Refresh The Bootstrap Cap. A Charge Pump (As On The DRV8353[11]) Keeps The High-Side FET Fully Enhanced Even At Full Duty, Which Matters When A Motor Is Loaded Near Top Speed. This Is General Power-Electronics Knowledge Reflected In The Datasheet Feature Set.

2 — The MOSFETs

Never Use A 48V-Rated MOSFET On A 48V Bus. The design rule is to size VDS to at least 1.5× the system voltage, which for 48V means 80V — and often 100V — rated parts[12].

The margin is not paranoia: switching a real motor's inductance against PCB and package parasitics produces overshoot and ringing on the phase node far above the nominal bus. This is standard power-stage behaviour, not a source-specific claim.

3 — The Control Scheme

Match The Brain To The Motor And To How Hard You Want To Work. Six-step is the least code and the least sensing; FOC is smoother and quieter but needs per-phase current sense and a faster loop. Both VESC and moteus implement FOC out of the box, which is why cloning one beats writing commutation from scratch.

04 Where 48V Bites

The Gotchas That Kill Boards

A 48V Bus Is High Enough To Destroy Silicon In Milliseconds And Low Enough To Tempt You Into Thin Margins. These are general engineering failure modes, called out so a first build survives.

SHOOT-THROUGH

If Both FETs In One Leg Conduct Together, The Bus Shorts To Ground Through Them And They Fail Almost Instantly. The Fix Is Dead-Time — A Deliberate Gap Where Both Are Off During The Hand-Off. Too Little Dead-Time Risks Cross-Conduction; Too Much Forces Body-Diode Conduction And Wastes Efficiency. Use The Gate Driver's Configurable Dead-Time Rather Than Relying On The MCU.

  • Power-Loop Layout — The High-di/dt Loop (Bus Cap → High-Side FET → Low-Side FET → Back To Cap) Must Be Physically Tiny. Wide Copper Pours, Minimal Loop Area, Many Parallel Vias, And The Bootstrap/Decoupling Caps Right At The Driver. Loop Inductance Is What Turns A 48V Bus Into 70V+ Spikes.
  • Regen Overvoltage — When The Motor Decelerates It Becomes A Generator And Pushes The Bus Above The Battery Voltage. A 48V System Can Climb Well Past Nominal On Hard Braking, So Your FET And Cap Ratings Must Absorb It (Or You Need A Brake Resistor / Clamp).
  • Bus Capacitance — Size The DC-Link Caps For The Ripple Current, Not Just The Voltage. Undersized Bulk Capacitance Lets The Bus Sag And Ring Under Fast Current Steps.

05 Licensing

The "Open" Traps

Open Firmware Does Not Mean You Can Ship It Under Any Name. Two traps catch people building on these projects.

  • VESC Trademark ≠ Copyright Licence — The GPLv3 Firmware And CC-BY-SA Hardware Do Not Grant Rights To The VESC® Name. Its Trademark Policy States That An Open-Source Copyright Licence "Does Not Include An Implied Right Or License To Use A Trademark," And That The Mark Must Not Appear On Cloned Products[6]. Build The Hardware Freely; Do Not Brand It "VESC."
  • ODrive Is Closing — Older v3.x Firmware Is Open, But The Source For The Current Pro / S1 / Micro Generation "Is Currently Not Publicly Available" (Access Only Under NDA), And The Matching Hardware Files Are No Longer Released[7]. It Is Fine As A Product; It Is Not A Foundation You Can Fully Own.

06 Open Questions

What The Research Couldn't Pin Down

A Few Facts Stayed Below The Bar Of A Verifiable Primary Source and are flagged rather than guessed:

CONFLICT — VESC 4.12 GATE DRIVER

Sources Disagreed On The Exact Part In The Older VESC 4.12: One Cited A Generic DRV8301, Another The DRV8302. Both Are The Same 6–60V TI Family; The Canonical VESC 4.12 Part Is The DRV8302. Not Load-Bearing For A 48V Build, Since The 48V Recommendation Is The VESC 6 MkVI, Not The 4.12.

UNRESOLVED NUMBERS

The Exact Regen Overvoltage Peak For A Given 48V Motor/Controller, The Minimum Bootstrap Hold-Up Time At 48V, And Whether VESC Publishes Complete Manufacturing Gerbers Versus Schematics Only — None Resolved To A Quotable Primary Source. Treat The Regen And Bootstrap Points As Qualitative Design Warnings, Not Design Numbers.
